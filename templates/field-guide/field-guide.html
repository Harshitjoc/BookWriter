Tomato and basil

A pairing, not two plants

You're looking at a tomato with basil crowded right up against its stem. That's a mistake most people make: basil likes a little room, and planting it as a companion means keeping it near, not on top.

Grown this way, the basil's smell confuses what's hunting the tomato, and the marigolds at the row's end pull pests toward the flowers instead. You keep the tomatoes.

Some plants are just easier to grow next to each other.

THIS WEEKEND

Basil at the base of each tomato, marigolds at both ends of the bed. Keep the basil about a hand's width out, never touching the stem.
