# Voice — one more winter

This is the voice for a book of short essays: storytelling with a turn. The `/block`
skill reads this file before it writes a word. If you copy this template, rewrite it in
your own voice, and only ever with your own true material.

## Who you are writing for

One reader, alone, willing to sit with a page. They are not looking for advice and
they will not finish a paragraph that is trying to impress them. They will finish a
paragraph that shows them something they half-knew.

## The rules

- **No em dashes.** Commas and full stops. It is the loudest machine-written tell.
- **Contractions, always.** "you're", "it's", "didn't".
- **One scene, one turn.** Open in a specific moment, not a thesis. The idea arrives
  last, and only after the reader has seen the thing it came from.
- **Concrete over abstract.** A cable drawer, a jacket that never gets worn. One real
  object does more than a sentence about "attachment".
- **Human psychology, not self-help.** Name the fear under the behaviour. Then stop.
  Do not tell the reader what to feel next.
- **Uneven rhythm.** A three-word sentence after a long one. Sameness reads as
  machine-composed.
- **Never present an invented story as fact.** A memoir that fabricates its life is a
  lie, and readers can smell it. Frame a hypothetical as a scenario ("Say you're…"),
  and put your own true events only where they belong: in your book.

## The opening

Begin inside the moment. Let the reader be standing there before they know what it
means.

> "Say you're going away for a week and you've packed for three."

## The middle

Show the behaviour, then the fear under it. Use the colour roles for the two moves:
the thing you notice (ink), the thing you're avoiding (red), the small release (green).

## The closer

One line the reader will carry out of the essay. It should land a beat later, not
explain itself.

> "Packing light isn't about less. It's about trusting the person who arrives."

## The action box

Label it as a small, private try: TRY THIS NEXT TRIP, NOTICE THIS WEEK, ONE HONEST
THING. It invites an experiment, never a moral.
