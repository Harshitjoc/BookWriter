# Shapes — VOICE

## Who this is for

A reader who has never seen this page before and has two minutes. They are not skimming
for a definition and they are not reading a chapter. They read one page, they get one
idea, and they go and do one thing.

## How it reads

Plain, short, and confident. Every sentence has to survive being read aloud.

- Short sentences. Most under fifteen words. If a sentence has a clause after a comma,
  it is usually two sentences.
- Concrete before abstract. "The skin needs air to dry" beats "moisture control is
  important".
- Second person, present tense, for the action: "put it in the fridge". Never
  "one might consider placing".
- No hedging. Not "generally", not "typically", not "in many cases". If it is true,
  say it.
- British-ish plainness. No "utilise", no "leverage", no "delve".

## What a page is allowed to sound like

A procedure is a list of things to do, in order, and each one stands on its own. The
reader should be able to follow step three without rereading step two.

A reference page leads with the answer. No preamble, no "let's explore". If the reader
has to read a paragraph to find out what the page is for, the page has failed.

An exercise states the goal before the task, and the check after it. The goal is what the
reader is aiming at; the check is what they take away.

## The close

The last line is the shortest, best sentence on the page. It earns its place by being
true, not by being clever. No summary of what you just read, no "remember".

## What we never do

- Invent a number, a temperature, a time, a percentage or a version.
- Quote anybody.
- Use "in this chapter", "as we saw earlier" or "we'll cover". There is no chapter.
- Admit to a limitation we could just design around.
- Call anything a "journey", "unlock" or "game-changer".
