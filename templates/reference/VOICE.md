# Voice — the pocket reference

This is the voice for a book someone opens to find one thing and then closes. It is
not a book you read from the front, and it does not pretend to be. The `/block` skill
reads this file before it writes a word. If you copy this template, rewrite it.

## Who you are writing for

Someone mid-task, with the book open in one hand. They are annoyed, they are in a
hurry, and they already half-know the answer. Your job is to confirm it and get out
of the way. Every sentence they do not need is a sentence that wasted their time.

## The rules

- **Answer in the first sentence.** Not the second. No "let's explore", no history
  unless the history is the thing they looked up.
- **No em dashes.** Commas and full stops. It is the loudest machine-written tell.
- **Contractions, always.** "it's", "you're", "doesn't".
- **Short. Then shorter.** If a paragraph runs past three sentences, it wanted to be
  a table row, not a paragraph.
- **The table is the page.** Put the detail in `<table class="ref">`, not in prose.
  A row the reader can scan beats a sentence they have to parse.
- **End with the gotcha.** The one thing that bites people. Say it plainly, once.
- **Never invent a fact, a number, or a version.** A reference that is wrong is
  worse than no reference. If you are unsure, leave the gap, do not guess.

## The opening

State the answer, then explain. The reader may stop after line one and that is fine.

> "The first digit is the whole story."
> "Sorting is stable here. Equal items keep their order."

## The middle

Lead with the rule, then the exception, then the trap. Use the colour roles: the
answer in ink, the trap in red, the safe form in green.

## The closer

One line the reader could repeat without the book. If a page can be summarized in a
sentence, that sentence is the closer.

## The action box

Label it for the moment: REMEMBER, GOTCHA, ONE RULE, CHECK FIRST. It holds the single
thing to carry away, not a summary of the page.
